# Vancouver Tree Journal

## Project Type
Protogen P303 Mobile Experience

## Product Philosophy
Build a trusted outdoor companion: a field guide, naturalist companion, exploration tool, and stewardship tool. This is not a dashboard, municipal system, or data-entry product. Help people feel welcome, capable, optimistic, and connected to Vancouver's shared urban forest.

This is an independent educational prototype, not an official City of Vancouver or Vancouver Park Board application.

## Audience and Context
Vancouver residents, volunteers, students, park visitors, and urban forest enthusiasts use the experience outdoors. Design for one-handed use, bright sunlight, distractions, and limited attention. Mobile is primary; desktop is secondary.

## Core Journey
Explore Trees → Identify a likely species → Learn something useful → Notice tree condition and wildlife → Save an observation.

Keep navigation shallow and make location, next action, and return path obvious. Primary destinations are Explore the Field Guide, Record an Observation, Map My Sightings, My Field Notes, and Notes from Under the Canopy.

## Species Priorities
Lead with Douglas Fir, Western Red Cedar, Bigleaf Maple, Arbutus, Sitka Spruce, and Western Hemlock. Include Cherry Trees, Japanese Maple, and London Plane as popular urban species. Feature Windmill Palm as a discovery species that reflects Vancouver's mild coastal climate and enthusiastic urban gardeners.

## Encyclopedia Profiles
Each concise profile includes:
- Hero photo, common name, and scientific name
- Description and typical Vancouver locations
- Identifying characteristics and seasonal clues
- What healthy looks like and warning signs
- Wildlife connections and an interesting fact

Keep the species name and primary action prominent. Avoid long essays. Educational guidance must not imply definitive identification or professional diagnosis.

## Observation Flow
Keep the task under two minutes with plain-language prompts and three overall choices: Healthy, Monitor, and Concern. A user can select a species, date, visible leaf/needle, bark and branch conditions, optional wildlife observations, and a short note. Wildlife choices: bird activity, nest present, pollinators observed, small mammals observed, and no activity observed. Make save confirmation clear and store observations locally on this device only.

## Urban Forest Storytelling
Explain the value of Vancouver's urban forest through shade, climate resilience, biodiversity, wildlife habitat, and neighbourhood well-being. Treat the canopy as a shared community asset and encourage practical stewardship.

## Accessibility and Visual Hierarchy
Prioritize photography, tree name, important information, actions, then supporting details. Use large tap targets, readable high-contrast text, simple labels, visible focus, keyboard-operable controls, reduced-motion support, and clear ways back. Design for all ages and avoid business-dashboard conventions.

## Data and Trust
Use local species content and individually attributed, reusable photos. State clearly that observations are private to the browser/device and are not sent to the City. Location is opt-in and requested only after a user action. The sightings map displays only the user's own geotagged notes; do not imply it is a verified tree inventory or municipal service. OpenStreetMap tiles require network access. Before adding external tree inventory/news data, document source, freshness, reuse terms, and limits. Treat health descriptions as observations, not arborist assessments.

## Success Criteria
A first-time user can identify a likely tree, learn something interesting, understand what healthy looks like, record and save an observation in under two minutes, and feel more connected to Vancouver's urban forest.
