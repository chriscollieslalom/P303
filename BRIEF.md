# Project Brief: Vancouver Tree Journal

## Summary
Vancouver Tree Journal is an independent educational prototype that helps people explore Vancouver's urban forest, learn about local trees, and take part in everyday stewardship.

It should feel like a trusted outdoor companion: a field guide, naturalist companion, and exploration tool. It is not a dashboard, municipal system, or data-entry product, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by the City of Vancouver or Vancouver Park Board.

## Audience and setting
Vancouver residents, volunteers, students, park visitors, and curious naturalists use the experience outdoors, often one-handed, in sunlight, with limited time and attention.

## Core journey
Explore trees → identify a likely species → learn one useful thing → notice signs of tree condition or wildlife → save a quick field observation.

The experience should make people feel welcome, capable, optimistic, and more connected to a shared community asset.

## Species priorities

Primary Vancouver species:
- Douglas Fir
- Western Red Cedar
- Bigleaf Maple
- Arbutus
- Sitka Spruce
- Western Hemlock

Popular urban species:
- Cherry Trees
- Japanese Maple
- London Plane

Discovery species:
- Windmill Palm, reflecting Vancouver's mild coastal climate and enthusiastic urban gardeners

## Species profiles
Every profile should make the common and scientific names easy to find and provide a hero photo, concise description, typical Vancouver locations, identifying characteristics, what healthy looks like, warning signs, wildlife connections, and an interesting fact. Content is educational, not a definitive identification or professional tree-health assessment.

## Observation experience
An observation should take less than two minutes. Use simple prompts and the status choices Healthy, Monitor, and Concern. Let people optionally note bird activity, a nest, pollinators, small mammals, or no activity observed. Make saving clear and reassuring; observations remain on the user's device in this prototype.

## Urban forest story
Show how trees contribute shade, climate resilience, biodiversity, wildlife habitat, and neighbourhood well-being. Frame care for the canopy as shared community stewardship, without implying official City or Parks Board affiliation.

## Design direction
Photography, tree name, and important information lead the hierarchy; actions follow, with supporting detail close by. Prioritize one-handed mobile use, large targets, high contrast, readable text, simple labels, keyboard access, and clear return paths. Keep the tone calm, educational, welcoming, and community-focused.

Design references: Merlin Bird ID, iNaturalist, Seek, AllTrails, and Parks Canada.
